Movement and tower defence
Buildings change the pursuit route
The player cannot attack directly, so a building has to offer something useful: a detour, broken sight lines or space to turn back towards the tower. Supplies near buildings draw the player away from the shortest route. The reward then has to justify that extra distance.
Returning with something useful
Materials pay for upgrades, while a repair parcel restores 15% of maximum tower health. Only one parcel can be carried. Delivery requires reaching within two units of the tower with a clear line of sight. If its health is full, the parcel stays available for later damage.

How a tower upgrade works
04 / TOXIC BARRIER
Pressing E near the tower starts this sequence: check the request, pay the materials, then apply the new level. The code below is from Tower.cs.
Tower.cs / TryUpgrade()
public bool TryUpgrade(PlayerInventory inventory)
{
    if (!CanUpgrade(inventory))
        return false;

    int targetLevel = NextLevel;
    TowerUpgradeCost cost = GetUpgradeCostForNextLevel();
    if (!TryPayUpgradeCost(inventory, cost))
        return false;

    SetLevel(targetLevel);
    return true;
}
Checking and paying for an upgrade
TowerUpgradePanelUI requests the upgrade. Tower.cs checks whether the player is close enough, the level is below the cap and the inventory covers the cost. CanUpgrade() can also tell the panel whether to enable its button; checking availability does not spend anything.
TryPayUpgradeCost() checks all four resource counts before consuming any materials. If teeth are missing, for example, it must not spend the available wood first. TryUpgrade() reaches SetLevel() only after payment succeeds.
The panel can query CanUpgrade() without spending resources. The purchase path checks again before payment, so a previously enabled button is not permission to spend. SetLevel() clamps the requested level and ignores an unchanged value, preventing the same level from replaying its healing and effects.
After SetLevel()
Combat stats and the sprite update. Both player and tower health refill, then sound, particles and a scale pulse announce the upgrade.
Level event
OnLevelChanged calls the upgrade panel’s RefreshAll(), updating the cost or max-level state after the purchase.
Region access — MapController.cs
if (mapLevel <= 1) return true;

MapController.cs keeps region 1 available from the start. Tower levels 2 and 3 unlock the outer rings. One upgrade therefore changes both the strength of the defence and the space the player can explore.

Finding the way back
Supplies appear after the player approaches them in an unlocked region. Actual terrain, region states and the tower position help compare routes home. Opening the map pauses combat, so planning a return does not cost tower health while the player reads.
Detours around buildings
A supply beside a building offers a reward for leaving the direct route. Collection requires a clear line of sight, so the player cannot take it through a wall. Turning around the building can separate them from a pursuer, but the return route still has to lead into tower range. I need to test whether that extra movement produces a deliberate choice.
1 / Reading the parasite warning
Parasites look like resource drops but cannot be collected. Contact deals repeated damage while the player remains over them. The red edge grows stronger with proximity rather than marking the exact trap. I want the player to slow down, move away and judge nearby pickups, instead of automatically collecting everything in reach.
2 / Deciding when to turn back
The player cannot attack directly. Bringing a pursuer into tower range gives movement an offensive purpose. A repair parcel adds another reason to return: one can be carried, and delivery requires being within two units of the tower with a clear line of sight. It restores up to 15% of maximum tower health and is kept if the tower is already full.
3 / The boss can damage either ally. Its ground warning gives time to dodge, but moving the player clear does not save a tower inside the strike. Watching only the player’s health misses half of the defence problem.

Finite supplies
Side-route supplies do not respawn. Before the final upgrade, ordinary supplies give materials, 35 health and 18 seconds of toxic protection. 02 / 06
The table, the cards & the shared supply
A personal board limits commitment
Each player has three active-project spaces and up to three permanent infrastructure symbols. New tasks wait in hand until Morning Meeting. Infrastructure stays available after a project is completed; it is not a one-use token. Tasks represent pedestrians, cyclists, students and vehicles, each asking for different combinations of the same limited safety symbols.
40 task cards
Nine designs turn safety requirements into points. Senior Group Crossing needs two Crosswalks and a Stop Sign for 5 SP.
16 traffic events
Eight designs change the conditions for a round. School Holiday affects Student tasks; Budget Freeze closes funding actions.
24 government cards
Eight policies provide ongoing effects. Gentrification Ordinance makes an opponent discard an incomplete task after its owner completes one.
6 Pedestrian + 6 Vehicle + 6 Emergency dice
Pedestrian faces supply Sidewalk, Crosswalk and Bike Lane symbols. Vehicle faces supply Stop Sign, Traffic Light and Speed Camera symbols. Each safety die has a Wild face restricted to its own type. Emergency dice create costs or penalties rather than filling safety requirements.
Starting set per player: 3 employees, 2 Coins and 3 task cards in hand. Across four players there are 12 employees. The Budget Office uses a separate funding die with results 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3. Coins, infrastructure and a round marker complete the shared supplies.

03 / 06
One round, three different decisions
Morning Meeting — commit before you draft
Reveal the round’s event, then move tasks from hand into open project slots. Players place their three employees one at a time. A space holds one pawn, so an early choice can deny an action to somebody else. Pay a space’s Coin cost when claiming it.
Rush Hour — choose only what you can use
Roll the available dice and draft by type. Take one eligible die at a time and place it immediately. A matching safety die needs an empty requirement on an active project. If no legal option exists, pass for that selection; you may still draft when another die type is resolved.
End of Day — resolve before you replenish
Mitigate or resolve emergencies, then score projects whose requirements are satisfied. Return dice from completed projects; leave safety dice on unfinished ones. Retrieve employees, gain 2 Coins and draw new tasks. The end-of-Round-4 draw is reduced to one card.
Repeat for five rounds. Most Safety Points wins; ties compare completed tasks, then remaining Coins.
Worked example: Weekend Explorer
This project needs one Bike Lane and one Sidewalk for 3 SP. Drafting the Bike Lane alone does not score it: that die stays on the task if the Sidewalk is still missing. A permanent Sidewalk symbol can satisfy the remaining requirement without being consumed.
Timing matters: finishing a task frees its space, but a replacement task enters during the next Morning Meeting. The empty slot is not a mid-draft opportunity to activate another project.
What an unfinished task costs
It occupies one of only three project slots and holds safety dice out of the pool. Progress is retained, but both the player’s available space and the shared supply become tighter.
Final score: completed-task SP + applicable policy bonuses + 1 SP per complete set of 3 Coins − unresolved emergency penalties.
Approaching Round 5: a short task can release dice and score before the game ends. A higher-value project may take too long. Keep enough Coins for emergencies, while weighing their final-scoring value.

Maintenance Yard
2 spaces · costs 1 / 2 Coins
Pay the space cost and discard a task for one permanent infrastructure symbol. Resolve at the end of Morning Meeting.
Legislative Chamber
3 spaces · costs 0 / 1 / 2 Coins
On placement: choose a visible Government card and refill the display. Policy holdings have no limit.
Mayor’s Office
2 free spaces · different players
The first and second placements secure first and second priority for the coming Rush Hour. Determine priority after employee placement.
Not every action waits: immediate funding can pay for a later placement. Public card displays change as players take cards. Maintenance and Mayor priority resolve later, so placement order and effect timing are separate decisions.
Worker placement
Three employees cannot cover all five departments. Secure scarce actions early: taking funding may mean losing a task or policy to another player. Balance immediate funding against reusable infrastructure. A paid space can still be worthwhile when it supports projects already active.
Dice drafting
Choose tasks with overlapping requirements. Keep Coins available to adjust a die or mitigate danger. Two Coins can turn a drafted non-emergency die to a chosen face; spending them now trades flexibility against end-game funding points.
Government policies
Time policy effects around contested actions and avoid relying on a single department. Inter-Agency Agreement can exchange a drafted die with another player’s die, so a plan based on one exact result may be vulnerable.

Making a readable physical game
Start with decisions and reading order
My early phase notes separate employee placement, drafting and scoring. The card sketch gives the task title, artwork, points and requirements distinct areas; policy and event cards reserve more room for their effect text. The layout follows what the player needs to check when choosing a card.
Use references, then select consistently
I develop prompts from sketches and style references, then use AI-generated illustrations. I select results against the approved card artwork: a warm city setting, consistent characters, similar rendering and readable subjects. The art process includes my selection and direction; the illustrations are AI-assisted.
Keep the rules outside the artwork
Titles, point values and safety requirements remain separate from the illustration. A player can check a cost or symbol without interpreting the scene. Card families also differ by header colour: blue tasks, red events and muted green policies.
The early paper version uses simpler components to put the turn sequence on the table. The printed version keeps the same need for visible action spaces, separate projects and a shared supply.
From screen to tabletop
Repeated fields help players scan cards side by side. The physical photographs show the printed cards, symbol dice and player boards together; reviewing these at table distance matters more than judging an isolated illustration at full screen.

Recover lost emotions in a world of consciousness. Masks provide the abilities used to cross platforms, survive enemies and confront the force that stole the character’s sense of self.
My role
Game programmer, sharing programming with one teammate in a team of five.
Team Chillers · Unity / C#
Controls & basic movement
Space jumps; Left Shift adds a short sprint burst. Left click attacks while a mask is equipped. Keys 1–3 select carried masks; 0 removes the active mask. A short coyote-time window accepts jumps just after leaving a platform edge.
GGJ project ↗ Gameplay video ↗
Six emotions; only three carried
The loadout contains at most three masks, with one active at a time. Owned masks stay in the collection; changing the carried set requires a rest point. Removing a mask away from rest costs 10 durability.
Abilities change the route and the risk
Anger boosts attack damage and reach, consuming durability per attack. Happiness enables wall sliding and wall jumping. Shame provides a second jump with a durability cost. Sadness spends durability to grant a shield when equipped.
Movement is also recovery
Anxiety repairs another carried mask while moving, spending its own durability. Silence heals while stationary. Recovery therefore asks for different spaces: a route to keep moving, or enough safety to stop.
Design reading: prepare, commit, recover
A three-slot loadout cannot cover every ability. Taking a movement mask, an attack mask and a recovery mask trades flexibility for a plan. Swapping helps with the next obstacle, but has a cost. Rest points restore health, repair masks and allow a different selection before the next level.
Spikes deduct health and return a surviving player to a checkpoint. Death routes through rest before returning to the failed level.
