PROGRESSION THROUGH SPACE
The three regions surround a fixed centre. The first ring keeps early gathering trips close to the tower. Reaching tower Lv.2 opens the second region; Lv.3 opens the third. The upgrade changes both the defence and the space the player can explore.
WHY LEAVE THE REFUGE?
Remaining near the tower is safer, so outward trips need a purpose. Resources fund upgrades and repair supplies help the tower survive. Leaving attack range creates a choice: keep collecting, or return before the journey becomes too dangerous.
FINAL OBJECTIVE  Reach tower Lv.3 and clear every wave and remaining enemy across all three regions.

How a tower upgrade works
04 / TOXIC BARRIER
Pressing E near the tower starts this sequence: check the request, pay the materials, then apply the new level. The code below is from Tower.cs.
Tower.cs / TryUpgrade()
public bool TryUpgrade(PlayerInventory inventory)
{
    if (!CanUpgrade(inventory))
        return false;

    int targetLevel = NextLevel;
    TowerUpgradeCost cost = GetUpgradeCostForNextLevel();
    if (!TryPayUpgradeCost(inventory, cost))
        return false;

    SetLevel(targetLevel);
    return true;
}
Why the panel does not spend materials
TowerUpgradePanelUI requests the upgrade. Tower.cs checks whether the player is close enough, the level is below the cap and the inventory covers the cost. CanUpgrade() can also tell the panel whether to enable its button; checking availability does not spend anything.
TryPayUpgradeCost() checks all four resource counts before consuming any materials. If teeth are missing, for example, it must not spend the available wood first. TryUpgrade() reaches SetLevel() only after payment succeeds.
The rule stays in Tower.cs, so rearranging the upgrade panel does not change how payment or levelling works.
After SetLevel()
The sprite changes, combat stats update and the upgrade effect plays. The UI refreshes to show the new level and the next cost.
Level event
OnLevelChanged notifies listeners. Other systems read the tower level instead of maintaining a second, independent upgrade count.
Unlocking region 2 and region 3
if (mapLevel <= 1) return true;
return tower != null && tower.CurrentLevel >= mapLevel;
MapController.cs keeps region 1 available from the start. Tower levels 2 and 3 unlock the outer rings. One upgrade therefore changes both the strength of the defence and the space the player can explore.

The red screen edge signals a nearby parasite. It resembles loot, so gathering is not always safe. The cue gives a reason to slow down and pay attention.
2 / Lead enemies back
The player cannot attack directly. Lead enemies into tower range, or use buildings to change the route and gain space before returning.
3 / Watch the tower too
The tower is also vulnerable. The final siege boss can threaten either ally, with a ground warning that allows time to move before impact.
Bringing back a repair parcel
A repair parcel restores up to 15% of the tower’s health when brought back. The player carries one at a time. If the tower is already at full health, the parcel is kept. I want the damaged tower to be a reason to cut a gathering trip short; the playtest feedback on the next page questions whether that pressure is strong enough.

Detours around buildings
Buildings block direct routes and sight lines. Supplies near them pull the player off the shortest path. This is meant to make positioning useful even without a player attack: go around the obstacle, collect the supply, then find a route back into the tower’s range.

Playtest
06 / TOXIC BARRIER
Feedback from two players, summarised below.
Player 1
The opening felt too easy. The tower was too powerful, so there was very little pressure on the player.
Player 2
The game went straight into play and felt too straightforward. This player also felt little pressure.
What I need to reconsider
The player is supposed to protect the tower, but the early balance may let the tower do too much on its own. That would also weaken the need to gather supplies or return quickly. Both comments point to a balance problem; the second also makes me question whether the opening gives players enough direction and choice.
Changes to try next — not yet retested
Tower damage and range
Keep the current opening as a comparison. Lower the starting tower’s damage in one version, then test a shorter range separately. Keep resources and enemy encounters the same so I can see which change makes players intervene.
The first wave
Try different arrival times and approach directions. Watch whether players reposition or lead enemies back into range, and ask what made them move. Increasing deaths alone would not show that the encounter is better.
The first objective
Try a short gather–return–upgrade goal, then a choice of routes. Ask players to explain their goal without prompting. If it still feels too straightforward, the route choices may need more work.
The current opening and first tower upgrade.
During the next playtest
I would note when the player first reacts to danger, whether the tower takes damage before the first upgrade, and what causes a return trip. These observations should help distinguish a balance problem from an unclear objective.
Afterwards, I would ask when they first felt pressure and what options they thought they had. The next version should keep the player–tower partnership and three-region progression.
